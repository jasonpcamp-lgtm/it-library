Lack of Control

No Sense of Progress

Permanent Firefighting

Human Behavior · Procrastination Spikes
Agile Doesn't Stop the Spike. It Shrinks It.
Humans naturally defer. The question is how much damage occurs when they do.
Waterfall / Long Timebox
Work drifts flat. Then panic. One catastrophic crunch at the end — too late to recover or incorporate feedback.
Agile / 2-Week Sprints
Same human behavior. Compressed into two weeks. Damage is contained. Learning happens at every spike — not just the last one.
The Point
Agile doesn't make us stop procrastinating. It makes sure that when we do, the damage is small and the learning is immediate.

Humphrey's Law
You Only Know What You Want When You See What You Don't
Requirements written in a conference room are hypotheses. Showing real work turns hypotheses into real feedback — fast.
The Long Way
A stakeholder describes what they need. The team builds for months. At the end, it's wrong. Not because anyone was careless — because nobody actually knew what "right" looked like until they saw something real. The cost of being wrong at month six is catastrophic.
The Short Loop
Build something small. Show it in two weeks. The stakeholder sees what they don't want — which tells you exactly what they do. Wrong at week two costs almost nothing. The feedback makes the next two weeks better. And the ones after that.
Humphrey's Law

People don't know what they want until they see what they don't. Short loops are the fastest path to right.

The 4 Pillars
What Agile Actually Is
Not a tool. Not a ceremony. A shift in what we value.
👥
Individuals and interactions over processes and tools Better processes and tools don't fix a broken system. The people closest to the work — talking to each other, solving problems together — do. Agile puts them first.
🚢
Working deliverables over comprehensive documentation People only know what they want when they see what they don't. The only way to find that out is to put something real in front of them. A working deliverable creates that conversation. A document never will.
🤝
Customer collaboration over contract negotiation Someone hands us a requirement and disappears. Six months later it's wrong. Agile replaces that handoff with a continuous conversation — so we build what's actually needed, not what was assumed.
🔄
Responding to change over following a plan The plan you make on day one will be wrong. That's not a failure — it's reality. What matters is building a team that can adapt, not one that's locked into defending a document.

Business Continuity · The Phoenix Project
When Was the Last Time You Truly Disconnected?
Agile didn't just make the team more resilient. It gave individuals their lives back.
Before
You're the only one who knows. Your phone doesn't stop on vacation. You come back to a disaster. Getting sick feels like a professional risk. The work is yours and yours alone.
The team depends on one person for critical knowledge. When that person is unavailable — for any reason — the work stops and the scrambling starts.
→
After
The team knows. The work is visible on a shared board. Someone can pick it up. You can actually leave — and not think about this place until you come back.
Swarming became real. When something urgent hit, the whole team moved toward it together. It took practice. It wasn't always comfortable. But no one carried it alone.

Swarming — when the whole team moves together toward what matters most — isn't just an efficiency mechanism. It's how good people stop burning out alone.

Daniel Pink · Drive
What You're Actually After
Agile doesn't impose these things. It creates the conditions for what you already want.
01
Mastery
The drive to get better at things that matter. Short iterations create fast feedback loops — the fastest path to skill development is doing real work and learning from it immediately, not waiting for a quarterly review.
02
Purpose
Knowing why the work matters. When work is visible, prioritized, and connected to outcomes — people understand their contribution. Hidden work is purposeless work. Patient care starts with making IT work visible.
03
Autonomy
The team decides how to attack the work. Agile moves leadership from directing tasks to setting goals. Teams become self-organizing. That's not a risk — that's the point. And it turns out, it's also what people actually want.

A Note to the Skeptics
Three Voices That Block Change
They are real. They are understandable. And they are worth naming out loud.
Voice of Judgement
Shuts down an open mind. Prevents us from seeing possibilities and learning from others. Shows up as: "We tried this before." "This won't work here." "That's not how we do things."
Voice of Cynicism
Blocks empathy and compassion. Makes it difficult to connect with others and their ideas. Shows up as: "Leadership just wants a buzzword." "Nothing ever really changes." "Who came up with this?"
Voice of Fear
Inhibits willingness to take risks. Prevents stepping into the unknown to create meaningful change. Shows up as: "What if I fail?" "What does this mean for my role?" "I don't want to be exposed."
The ask is not certainty. The ask is two weeks of honest effort and open eyes.
