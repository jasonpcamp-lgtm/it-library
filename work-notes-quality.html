Work Notes
Quality

Seven questions every IT technician should be able to answer before closing, re-routing, reassigning, or escalating a ticket. Not a checklist — a discipline.

The Seven Fields
01 Symptoms Described
"What exactly is happening?"
Forces the technician to document observable reality — not assumptions, not causes. Errors, behaviors, and conditions that someone else could recognize without having been there.
02 Business Impact Explained
"Who is hurting, and how badly?"
Anchors the ticket to real-world consequence. Scope, severity, and timing tell leadership, on-call teams, and vendors whether this warrants urgency — without them having to ask.
03 Configuration Items (CIs) Affected
"What systems are involved?"
Names the infrastructure at stake. CI names allow anyone picking up the ticket to immediately identify what is in scope — and what downstream dependencies may be at risk.
04 Actions Completed
"What have we already tried — and what happened?"
Prevents duplicate effort and documents institutional knowledge. Results matter as much as the actions — a step that failed narrows the problem space just as much as one that succeeded.
05 Actions Planned
"What happens next, who owns it, and when?"
Ensures the ticket has forward motion. A work note without a next step is a dead end. Owner and ETA make accountability visible — to the team, to the customer, and to anyone reviewing later.
06 Timestamp Present
"When did this happen, and when was this written?"
Time-anchors the narrative. System timestamps record when the note was logged; contextual timestamps establish the actual timeline of events — critical for incident reconstruction and SLA review.
07 People Involved Identified
"Who did what?"
Puts names and roles on the record. Key contacts, responding teams, vendors, and on-call personnel — documented so that handoffs are clean, accountability is clear, and follow-up goes to the right person.
