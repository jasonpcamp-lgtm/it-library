The demo counts each job's time from the start until it is finished. Running the short jobs first cuts the average from 115 ms to 47.5 ms, about 59% less, with the same total work. The big job finishes 30 ms later, but three jobs finish 90 ms or more sooner.

The reason is simple: whatever runs first makes every job behind it wait. Put a long job at the front and its whole length is added to each job in the queue. Put the short ones at the front and the queue clears quickly. When all the jobs are known up front, shortest-first gives the lowest average wait of any order.

The catch: if new short jobs keep arriving, a big job can be pushed back forever. Teams need a way to weigh it again over time, which is where WSJF comes in.

From Shortest Job First to WSJF

Shortest Job First assumes every job is equally worth finishing. Real work isn't. A small task may be worth little, and a big one may be urgent. Weighted Shortest Job First (WSJF) keeps the idea but weighs each job by what it costs to wait for it.

WSJF = Cost of Delay ÷ Job Size

Do the job with the highest score first.

  • Cost of Delay: what waiting costs. In SAFe (Scaled Agile Framework) it is scored as business value + time criticality + risk reduction or opportunity enablement, each on a relative scale.
  • Job Size: how long it takes, as a relative estimate. It stands in for duration.

If every job has the same Cost of Delay, WSJF puts them in size order, which is Shortest Job First. If every job is the same size, it puts the most costly delay first. Time criticality also answers the catch above: a job that waits gets more urgent, so its score rises when the backlog is re-scored.

A worked example

Three features. Treat Cost of Delay as cost per week of waiting and Job Size as weeks of work.

FEATURECOST OF DELAYJOB SIZEWSJF
Reporting overhaul20201.0
Login fix824.0
Alert routing1352.6

Ranking by value alone starts with the reporting overhaul, since it has the highest Cost of Delay. WSJF starts with the login fix, then alert routing, then the overhaul.

ORDERDONE AT (WEEKS)DELAY COST
Highest value first: overhaul, alerts, login20, 25, 2720×20 + 13×25 + 8×27 = 941

Same three features, same 27 weeks. The WSJF order runs up about 31% less cost of delay, because the small, valuable items stop waiting behind the big one.

These numbers are an illustration. In practice the scores are relative estimates, so use WSJF to compare and order work, not as a precise cost.
