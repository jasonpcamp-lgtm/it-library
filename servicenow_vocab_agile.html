SERVICENOW & AGILE — VOCABULARY REFERENCE

Agile & ServiceNow Vocabulary

This page defines common Agile and ServiceNow terms as they are used operationally. The goal is shared understanding, consistent language, and faster onboarding.

Foundational Concepts
01

Technical Debt

Technical debt is the accumulated cost of skipped fixes, unused capabilities, or delayed upgrades that make future change harder, slower, or more expensive. It is the price paid for shortcuts.

“Today’s quick fix is tomorrow’s slowdown.”
02

Agile Hierarchy

The Agile Hierarchy represents how work flows from strategy to execution.

Product → Theme (Why) → Epic (What) → Story (How) → Task (Steps) → Outcome
Agile Roles
03

Product Owner

Owns and grooms the backlog on behalf of customers and users. Ensures work is prioritized based on value and outcomes.

04

Scrum Master

Plans and administers all Scrum activities. Removes impediments, protects flow, and enables continuous improvement.

Core Agile Artifacts
05

Story

A small, user-focused requirement that delivers value and can be completed within a single sprint. Stories can have multiple tasks and multiple assignees.

06

Epic

A large body of work representing a major feature or enhancement. Epics span multiple sprints and are broken down into stories.

07

Theme

The highest level of the requirements hierarchy. Themes group epics by strategic goal or product capability.

08

Task

A discrete piece of work required to complete a story. Tasks exist to coordinate execution—not to add scope.

Planning & Delivery
09

Release

A fixed timeframe representing when a set of features is planned for delivery. Releases help establish capacity, sequencing, and expectations.

10

Unified Backlog

A single backlog containing stories, defects, incidents, and enhancements. Enables unified prioritization without record conversion.

Flow & Metrics
11

Velocity Chart

Displays historical delivery performance to help forecast future capacity.

12

Burndown Chart

Shows remaining work compared to ideal progress during a sprint.

This page is intended as a shared language reference. Precision in terminology drives better outcomes.
