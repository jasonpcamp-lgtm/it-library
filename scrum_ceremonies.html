01
Daily Stand-up
15 min · Every day · Sprint Goal alignment
▾
Scrum Master Context The stand-up is not a status report to management — it is a team synchronization for the Sprint Goal. Three questions drive it: Are we on track? Is anyone blocked? Who needs help? Your job is to protect the time box and move impediments offline immediately.
Role
✓ Do
× Don't
Product Owner
  • Listen for Sprint Goal risk
  • Note follow-ups
  • Clarify priority quickly if asked
Hears "waiting on access" and says: "That risks the goal — I'll escalate right after this."
  • Assign work
  • Status-manage the team
Scrum Master
  • Ask: On track? Blocked? Who needs help?
  • Keep it time-boxed (15 min)
  • Capture impediments immediately
Asks: "What's stopping this card from moving today?" and captures the action offline — does not solve it in the standup.
  • Problem-solve live in the meeting
  • Speak for the team
Team Member
  • Update your card before or during
  • Share progress toward Sprint Goal
  • Raise blockers clearly and directly
"Blocked waiting on firewall rule — moved card to Blocked." Done.
  • Hide issues or minimize blockers
  • Dive into solution details
02
Sprint Planning
2–4 hrs · Start of sprint · Capacity & commitment
▾
Scrum Master Context Planning is where the team commits to a Sprint Goal — not a task list. Your role is to protect capacity from scope creep and ensure stories are ready before the team pulls them. Push back early; a bloated sprint fails the team and the Product Owner.
Role
✓ Do
× Don't
Product Owner
  • Explain the why behind each story
  • Present an ordered, ready backlog
  • Answer questions — be available
Explains: "This story reduces Service Desk handoffs — that's the value. Everything else is lower priority."
  • Pre-commit scope or dates
  • Push stories that aren't ready
Scrum Master
  • Facilitate scope vs. capacity conversation
  • Protect the team's bandwidth
  • Ensure a clear Sprint Goal exits planning
Pushes back: "We're already at capacity — what do we trade off to add this story?" Forces the decision rather than absorbing it silently.
  • Push commitment beyond capacity
  • Let stories enter without acceptance criteria
Team Member
  • Pull only what the team can realistically finish
  • Ask questions before committing
  • Flag risks and dependencies early
Team says: "We can take 3 stories, not 5 — two engineers are out Thursday and Friday."
  • Commit without understanding the work
  • Stay silent on risks
03
Backlog Refinement
1 hr · Mid-sprint · Readiness for next sprint
▾
Scrum Master Context Refinement is about shared understanding — not just sizing. The team should leave knowing what "done" looks like for each story. If confusion persists after discussion, the story is not ready. Your job is to facilitate clarity, not own it.
Role
✓ Do
× Don't
Product Owner
  • Order and prioritize the backlog
  • Provide acceptance criteria before the meeting
  • Own the backlog — come prepared
Re-orders backlog after learning a dependency will delay another team — adjusts before the team pulls the wrong thing.
  • Skip refinement or show up unprepared
  • Present stories without acceptance criteria
Scrum Master
  • Facilitate — keep discussion focused
  • Surface confusion before it becomes a blocker
  • Ask: "What would done look like here?"
Notices the team is debating implementation rather than scope — redirects: "Let's first agree on what the story delivers. How can we implement it later."
  • Own the backlog or write acceptance criteria
  • Let unclear stories pass into planning
Team Member
  • Ask questions — surface confusion now, not in sprint
  • Suggest splitting large stories
  • Flag risks and dependencies early
Suggests splitting a story: "Config and automation are two different efforts — if we split it, we can ship config this sprint."
  • Defer questions to planning
  • Accept a story you don't understand
04
Sprint Retrospective
1 hr · End of sprint · Inspect & adapt
▾
Scrum Master Context Retro is your ceremony. Create psychological safety first — without it, nothing real surfaces. Redirect blame from people to systems. Drive toward at least one concrete, actionable improvement. Capture it as a Kaizen card and make it visible. Keep scope to this sprint — not the organization.
Role
✓ Do
× Don't
Product Owner
  • Listen openly — this is feedback, not a report
  • Support improvements the team commits to
  • Acknowledge your own impact on the sprint
Says: "I didn't realize late story changes caused rework — I'll lock acceptance criteria 48 hours before sprint start."
  • Defend past decisions
  • Redirect blame outward
Scrum Master
  • Create safety — silence signals fear, not agreement
  • Shift blame from people to systems
  • Drive to one actionable improvement (Kaizen card)
  • Focus on this sprint — not org-wide issues
Redirects: "Let's focus on the process, not the person. What about how we handle change requests could we improve?" Then creates a Kaizen card before the meeting ends.
  • Let blame settle on an individual
  • End without a committed action item
  • Let discussion drift to issues outside the sprint
Team Member
  • Be honest — say what went wrong and what worked
  • Commit to one specific improvement
  • Stay and contribute — presence matters
Commits: "I'll update cards daily, not just at standup — that was my gap this sprint."
  • Stay silent on what went wrong
  • Commit to change without intending to follow through
Ceremonies work when roles are clear and communication is honest. This guide is a starting point — not a script.
